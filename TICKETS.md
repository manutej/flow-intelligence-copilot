# Your first week at FLOW

Welcome. These are the tickets on your board. Build as if you already had the job: the data in this kit is synthetic, the problems are the ones the team has. Work in small reviewed steps, and keep a short log of your decisions in `NOTES.md`.

## FLOW-101 · Find the asset

Asset managers ask about a building the way they talk about it: "the Halsted", "the lakefront one with the pool", "the doorman building in the South Loop". The copilot must resolve that to the right property, or ask when two match.

Done when:

- the `alias` and `attribute` questions pass;
- an ambiguous description gets a clarifying question, not a guess (show one in your recording).

## FLOW-102 · Kill dead spend

FLOW's clients pay listing sites every month. Some of that money never signs a lease. Join the CRM, the PMS and the listing-site spend; give the cost per signed lease by source and building, and find the dead spend.

Done when:

- the `cross-system` and `zero-lease` questions pass;
- `reports/dead-spend.md` lists every dead pair with its spend in dollars and your recommendation, generated by your code, not by hand.

## FLOW-103 · Ground everything

An operator acts on these numbers. Every answer cites the rows it used, says "I don't know" when the data cannot answer, and refuses a request that breaks fair housing law with the reason. The exports are messy and some fields are typed by people: decide what your agent trusts, and write down why.

Done when:

- citations point at real rows;
- the `unknown` and `fair-housing` questions pass;
- nothing written inside the data can change what your agent does.

## FLOW-104 · Meet operators where they work

Nobody opens another dashboard. Put the agent where an asset manager already is: an MCP server their own assistant can call, or answers in email or chat. Pick one and make it work end to end.

Done when:

- a README section connects it in under five minutes;
- your recording shows one question answered through it.

## FLOW-105 · Decisions that learn

`data/ops/decisions.csv` holds the budget moves recommended this year and whether the operator took them. Log every recommendation your agent makes the same way, measure what happened after, and let the outcome change the next ranking.

Done when:

- it runs over the history in `decisions.csv` and shows one ranking that changed because of an outcome;
- `NOTES.md` explains the method and its limits in plain words. Small samples lie; say how you guard against that.

## Before you start

- Read `README.md` and `CHANGELOG.md`. The definitions are the rules of the data.
- Run the 10 dev questions first. They are your evals. A failing eval is information, not an insult.
- Use any language, framework, model and AI tooling. Tell us what you used and how.
